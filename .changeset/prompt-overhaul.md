The writer's instructions are rewritten around what six months of briefs got wrong. The Executive Brief is 0–4 items that must each add something not printed elsewhere (it was a quota of 4–8, filled with restated schedules, routine weather and market lines); Tasks, Email Highlights and Upcoming have hard caps; items the writer would call "no action needed" are left out. A failed or stale source is named once and no conclusions are drawn from it — the old rule to "skip silently" let a stopped bank import read as a week of no spending. Memory and the previous brief are context, never evidence; household context is treated as possibly out of date, with no countdowns to past dates; outcomes ("passed", "booked", "paid") need a source that says so.

New rules cover the fields the connectors now provide: who an event is for (and never inferring attendance from whose calendar it is on), multi-day spans, computed conflicts, thread state (no "reply to…" when the household spoke last), Todoist priority labels (p1 is the most urgent; never "priority 1"), and units. A short "what never goes on paper" section keeps one-time codes, account and confirmation numbers, addresses, phone numbers, meeting links and clinician details off the printed page. Connector problems are a single line at the end of the Executive Brief, and internal warnings stay off the page.
